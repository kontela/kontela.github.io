Ten candidate unrecorded connections between published cuneiform tablets

Produced by exhaustive pairwise comparison of rare 4-gram transliteration sequences across all 135,198 tablets in the CDLI ATF corpus, after excluding every pair already known (same composite, same composition designation, CDLI cross-references). Method validation: the same pipeline independently re-found 36 pairs that are cross-referenced on CDLI, including the MCS 1, 54 // MCS 6, 10 duplicate recorded only in Gomi, Orient 20 (1984). Six of the ten pairs below interlock into one ED IIIb Girsu é-munus ration-list cluster (barley rations under Sasa, wife of Urukagina; igi-nu-du₈ workers) scattered across Berlin, Harvard, Brussels, Paris, St. Petersburg and a private collection — reconstructed here purely from shared text.

Verification performed (2026-07-04)

  • CDLI artifact pages (both pages per pair, scraped): no reference to the partner tablet by P-number, museum number, or publication designation (zero-padding-tolerant matching).
  • Dahl, CDLB 2003:5 "A Note on Ur III Text Duplicates": none of these pairs appear (article covers Umma/Drehem gaba-ri kišib-ba copies). Note §6: "a renewed study of the entire body of Ur III administrative texts … will provide us with numerous examples" — the prediction this pipeline operationalizes.
  • Web co-mention search (Google, per pair, both designations + museum numbers): no source mentioning both members of any pair together.
  • BDTNS (checked 2026-07-04; full records retrieved for all six Ur III tablets): none of the three Ur III pairs is cross-referenced there; Remarks fields empty. The records also date each pair to the same year, nearby months: TUT 154 (Amar-Suen 2 iii, Berlin) ↔ Erm 14976 (Amar-Suen 2 i, St. Petersburg — whose only edition is Koslova's unpublished 2009 manuscript); Nik 2, 329 (Amar-Suen 5 vii, Moscow) ↔ SAT 2, 884 (Amar-Suen 5 v, Yale); CTNMC 53 (references Šulgi 31+33, Copenhagen) ↔ PPAC 5, 602 (Šulgi 33, British Museum). I.e. these read as scattered instalments of the same periodic account series rather than duplicates. (BDTNS does not cover ED IIIb, so the Girsu é-munus cluster cannot be checked there.)
  • Not yet checked: print commentaries (esp. Selz AWEL/FAOS 15 for the ED IIIb Girsu cluster, Prentice, Karahashi). That is exactly what a specialist can answer in minutes — hence the draft email.

Interpretation caution: for ration lists, hundreds of shared rare sequences can mean (a) true duplicate/copy, (b) consecutive periodic accounts of the same institution with the same personnel — an archival join across museums — or (c) fragments of one original account. All three are substantive; none are recorded for these pairs.
